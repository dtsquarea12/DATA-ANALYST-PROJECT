What the data shows

Gasoline, 95 octane. Subsidy totals compare the January to February average with the average since March.

Crude rose 65%. Saudi and Kuwaiti pump prices rose 0%. Brent averaged $70.89 in February and $116.80 in September. Saudi Arabia still charges SAR 2.33 and Kuwait 105 fils, the same as before the war.
The UAE passed the shock on. Its price resets monthly with the world market and is up 80% on its pre-war average. At AED 4.28 ($1.17 a litre) in October, UAE drivers pay about what Americans do ($1.15 in September).
The subsidy grew without any policy change. For the five GCC states the gasoline gap went from about $4bn a year at pre-war prices to about $16bn a year since March. Saudi Arabia accounts for $12bn of it.
Iran is a different case. Petrol costs one to four US cents a litre at the open-market exchange rate. The price gap implies roughly $44bn a year on gasoline alone, the least certain figure here.

Method and limits

How the subsidy is measured

This is the price-gap approach used by the IEA and the IMF. A subsidy exists when the pump price is below what the fuel would fetch on the open market.

gap per litre = reference price × (1 + VAT) − pump price
annual subsidy = gap per litre × litres sold per year

Pre-war is the average of January and February 2026. The war began on 28 February.

Two reference prices

  • Regional market: the UAE pump price before VAT. The UAE deregulated in 2015, so its price is what a Gulf driver pays with no state support. It tracked Singapore spot gasoline plus distribution costs within a few cents from May to August. By construction the UAE gap is zero.
  • International spot: EIA US Gulf Coast spot price plus $0.185 a litre for distribution, the midpoint of the IMF range of $0.15 to $0.22. It gives higher estimates, because US spot ran above Asian spot this year. Available to September.

What to keep in mind

  • Annual totals use 2023 sales volumes (OAPEC) and one grade per country. Treat them as orders of magnitude.
  • Diesel totals apply the pump price to all gasoil use, including industry, so they overstate.
  • Iran uses the middle quota tier (30,000 rials) at the open-market rate, and only months with a dated rate. Its price change is shown in rials, where it has not moved. Volumes include fuel that is smuggled out.
  • China, Australia and Canada are single-day readings, not monthly averages. Nigeria has no national figure after May. Kuwait's fourth-quarter price is not yet published.
  • Grades differ: 95 octane in the Gulf and Europe, regular in North America.
